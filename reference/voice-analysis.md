## Findings

1.  **Greeting**
    -   `Hi [First name],` is the dominant named-person greeting.
    -   `Hi,` is common when writing a general company inbox.
    -   `Dear [Team/Name],` appears when the recipient is institutional
        or the message is deliberately more formal.
    -   In this sample, roughly two thirds use `Hi` and roughly one
        third use `Dear` or a more formal team greeting.
2.  **Sign-off**
    -   `Kind regards,` plus `Gupenrakuru Kasinathan` is the strongest
        cold-outreach default.
    -   `Best regards,` appears in advice/networking outreach.
    -   `Many thanks` plus `Gupen` appears once rapport already exists.
    -   You normally sign your name. First contacts favor
        `Gupenrakuru Kasinathan`; established contacts often get
        `Gupen`.
3.  **Opening move**
    -   Most common: immediate self-context, for example,
        `My name is Gupenrakuru Kasinathan. I am a second-year...`
    -   Second: relationship/context hook, for example,
        `I recently attended your... event`.
    -   Third: follow-up framing, for example,
        `I am following up regarding...` or `Just following up...`
4.  **Sentence length and rhythm**
    -   Mixed rhythm, with medium-length explanatory sentences followed
        by short direct sentences.
    -   Cold outreach tends to use longer, information-dense sentences.
    -   Follow-ups become much shorter and more conversational.
    -   Fragments are uncommon in polished cold outreach but appear
        naturally in established threads.
5.  **Contractions**
    -   You use both forms.
    -   Cold outreach often uses `I am` for the introduction and factual
        claims.
    -   Warmer or established-contact messages use `I'm`, `I've`, `I'd`,
        and `you're` much more freely.
    -   The pattern is contextual rather than a strict preference for
        one form.
6.  **Formality**
    -   Overall: about 3.5/5.
    -   Cold company outreach: 4/5.
    -   Networking to an individual: 3 to 3.5/5.
    -   Existing mentor/contact follow-ups: 2.5 to 3/5.
    -   You become noticeably less formal after rapport exists.
7.  **Punctuation and formatting**
    -   Em dashes appear occasionally in older outreach, but they are
        not essential to the voice and should be removed from the AI
        spec.
    -   Exclamation marks are rare in first-contact professional
        outreach and more likely after rapport.
    -   Emoji are effectively absent from the professional sample.
    -   Bullets are uncommon unless the recipient asks for structured
        information.
8.  **Hedging vs directness**
    -   You are direct about the purpose but soften the ask.
    -   Typical softeners include `If there is any scope`,
        `I was wondering if`, `I would be grateful`,
        `I would really appreciate`, and `if possible`.
    -   You rarely use aggressive sales-style CTAs.
9.  **CTA examples**
    -   `If there is any scope for an internship or a conversation about future opportunities, I would really appreciate the chance to discuss it.`
    -   `I would welcome the chance to contribute to your projects and learn from the team.`
    -   `If there is any scope for an internship or junior role, I would be grateful for the chance to discuss it.`
    -   `I was wondering if you'd be open to sharing some advice on how to approach this.`
    -   `I would be grateful if you could let me know the next steps for booking the interview.`
10. **Humor, self-deprecation, warmth**

-   Humor is essentially absent from professional first-contact emails.
-   Self-deprecation is also absent.
-   Warmth comes through gratitude and genuine interest:
    `I really appreciate`, `would mean a lot`, `I find exciting`, and
    `I would welcome the opportunity`.
-   The tone is earnest rather than witty.

11. **Length**

-   The six fully representative substantive emails measured for the
    detailed length check had a median of about 180 words.
-   The broader sample includes shorter follow-ups, so 180 words is best
    treated as the typical substantial-outreach target rather than a
    universal median.
-   A 220-word hard cap gives enough headroom without drifting into
    essay length.

12. **Recurring phrases and verbal tics**

-   `I wanted to reach out`
-   `I am writing to ask`
-   `I was wondering`
-   `I would welcome the chance`
-   `I would really appreciate`
-   `If there is any scope`
-   `Thank you for your time`
-   `Many thanks`
-   `Please let me know`
-   `really interested`
-   `keen`
-   Frequent transition logic is simple: recipient relevance, then your
    evidence, then the ask.

13. **Phrases not characteristic of your professional voice**

-   No evidence in the sample for `I hope this email finds you well`,
    `circle back`, `touch base`, `leverage synergies`,
    `low-hanging fruit`, `move the needle`, `game-changer`,
    `uniquely positioned`, or `diverse skill set`.
-   No evidence of `Dear Sir/Madam` or sales-heavy language.
-   No evidence of routine emoji use in professional outreach.
-   Because absence cannot prove you have never used a phrase anywhere,
    these are best treated as sample-based banned phrases rather than
    literal lifetime claims.
